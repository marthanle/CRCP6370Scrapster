# Scrapster — Product Brief

## Problem

Busy students and young professionals let food go to waste — not from carelessness, but because turning random leftovers into an actual meal takes mental effort they don't have time for between class, work, and unpredictable schedules. For this group, it hits hardest as wasted money on a tight budget, plus daily decision fatigue ("what's for dinner?") on top of an already full plate.

## Target user

Busy students and young professionals (roughly 18–30):
- Living alone or with roommates, cooking for one or two
- Tight budgets where wasted food actually stings
- Unpredictable schedules that lead to forgotten groceries
- Comfortable using photo/app-based input over manual data entry

## Solution

An app where you input what you have — a photo of your fridge/pantry/ingredients or a typed list — and it builds a simple, low-equipment recipe around your most urgent ingredients first (the wilting spinach before the shelf-stable rice), combining several small leftovers into one coherent dish rather than just filtering by a single ingredient.

## How AI does the real work (not just a database lookup)

- Parses loose, messy input (a blurry photo or casual list, not a rigid dropdown)
- Ranks ingredients by spoilage urgency
- Reasons across multiple odds-and-ends to find a recipe that plausibly unifies them
- Learns dietary needs/preferences over time to rerank suggestions
- Remembers leftovers post-cooking to chain the next meal

## Key differentiator vs. existing apps (SuperCook, Yummly, etc.)

Those apps match ingredients to recipes. This app prioritizes by urgency and combination, defaults to simple/low-equipment recipes for this user's kitchen reality, and closes the loop with a visible savings/waste-reduced tracker — turning "I saved money and time" into a concrete, trackable number.

## Core features

1. Flexible input (photo or list)
2. Urgency-ranked recipe matching, tuned to simple/low-equipment cooking
3. Missing-ingredient shortlist → Budget mode (cheapest place to grab it)
4. Dietary/allergy personalization
5. Leftover chaining (multi-day meal plans from one grocery trip)
6. Savings/waste tracker (leads with money + time saved; sustainability as bonus framing)

## Business model

- Freemium: free basic matching; paid tier for meal-plan chaining, nutrition tracking
- Affiliate commission on grocery delivery for missing ingredients
- Potential B2B: campus dining/meal-plan partnerships, grocery chain loyalty-card integration

## Go-to-market

Campus-first launch — dorm/first-apartment kitchens, meal-swipe fatigue, shared-fridge mystery leftovers. Word-of-mouth + social content ("look what I made from basically nothing") fits how this age group discovers apps. Expand later to broader young-professional/budget households.

## Roadmap / future vision

Skill-adaptive step-by-step cooking guidance (image confirmation mid-recipe) — flagged as future, not core MVP, due to technical complexity.

## Open decision for the group

Whether the app assumes a dorm-style kitchen (microwave, minifridge, maybe hot plate) or a first-apartment kitchen (stove, oven) — worth picking one scenario clearly rather than trying to cover both.

## How it fits together end-to-end

1. You input what you have (photo or list)
2. AI flags what's urgent/about to expire
3. AI finds a recipe that uses the most/most urgent ingredients together
4. Tells you what's missing + cheapest place to grab it (Budget mode)
5. Learns your dietary needs to rerank options
6. After you cook, remembers leftovers and suggests the next meal (Leftover chaining)
7. Tracks savings/waste-reduced over time as the ongoing hook to keep opening the app
